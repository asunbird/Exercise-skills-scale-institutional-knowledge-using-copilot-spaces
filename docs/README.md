# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

## Overview
OctoAcme manages projects through a repeatable lifecycle designed to turn ideas into actionable delivery plans, maintain stakeholder alignment, and improve operational outcomes over time. The team follows a customer-first, iterative model that emphasizes clear ownership, measurable results, and continuous learning. These process documents provide practical guidance for planning, execution, release, and continuous improvement.

## Core project management processes
OctoAcme uses a five-phase lifecycle: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Close & Retrospective. Initiation validates the business need, defines success metrics, identifies stakeholders, and determines whether work should proceed. Planning turns approved initiatives into a prioritized backlog with milestones, dependencies, estimates, and a Definition of Done. Execution covers daily coordination, delivery tracking, testing, risk monitoring, and stakeholder communication. Release uses pre-release checks, deployment verification, and rollback readiness to reduce production risk. Retrospectives capture lessons learned and convert them into owned, time-bound improvements.

## Roles and responsibilities
Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications. Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the roadmap, and measure success against customer and business goals. Developers design, implement, test, and review features against acceptance criteria. QA and testing roles validate behavior and quality before release. Stakeholders and sponsors provide direction, prioritization, and approvals.

## Communication and quality practices
Teams use daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, milestone demos, and regular stakeholder updates to maintain visibility. Status updates cover progress, next steps, risks, blockers, and decisions needed. Quality is built into delivery through unit tests, integration tests, end-to-end smoke tests, security scanning, CI validation, and manual QA when needed. Risks and dependencies are tracked in a Risk Register and escalated from the team to the Project Manager, Product Lead, and sponsor according to business impact.
